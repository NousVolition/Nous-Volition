The first result: shape matters through connections

The Koch boundary and a matching 48-SIMS ring produced identical outcomes. Each SIM has the same two neighbors in both drawings. The Menger network changes the contacts: ordinary agreement occurred in 71.48% of its 20-SIMS rounds, compared with 52.54% on a 20-SIMS ring.

These are the main runs with no spontaneous initiative. Whole-network connectivity changes in the Menger comparison; the experiment does not isolate a uniquely fractal cause. Menger-versus-Koch would also change group size.

A resisting position can change the path

“Hold” keeps one SIM's starting color. “Oppose” chooses the opposite of its neighbors' majority when active. On Menger corner-opponent runs, 38.48% reached agreement at least once, but only 1.95% were unanimous at 120 seconds. First arrival and persistence answer different questions.

Batch 1 · check the dynamics before interpreting them

The first set covers the Lorenz orbit, phase space, damping, Euler's method, logistic growth, and a scalar laser-inspired threshold. Exact solutions and step refinement provide checks on numerical accuracy.

The scalar threshold uses the chosen equation n′=(p−1)n−n². It tracks an intensity-like quantity with growth and saturation. The improved laser in batch 2 includes a separate inversion variable. Optical phase is absent from both models.

Menger counts, volume, and exposed surface are checked through level 3. Koch counts, perimeter, and area are checked through level 5. Finite drawings approximate limiting fractal constructions. See Caltech's Menger notes and Reed's snowflake derivation.

Batch 3 · when motion keeps repeating

A stable limit cycle attracts nearby motion. An unstable cycle repels it. A half-stable cycle attracts from one side and repels from the other. We tested explicit radial examples on both sides of the unit circle; the unstable outer trajectory stops just before radius 2 to avoid its finite-time escape.

When a closed orbit is impossible

For your gradient example, x′=sin y and y′=x cos y, the potential V=−x sin y satisfies dV/dt=−sin²y−x²cos²y. A nonconstant closed orbit would have to return to its original potential despite losing potential along the way, which is impossible.

When a closed orbit must exist

For the stable radial example, flow points into the annulus 0.5≤r≤1.5 on both boundaries and never stops inside. This supplies the trapping and no-equilibrium conditions for Poincaré–Bendixson. The annulus has a hole; it does not meet Dulac's simply connected domain condition.

Sel’kov: a stable state can give way to a rhythm

x′=−x+ay+x²y   ·   y′=b−ay−x²y

The equilibrium is (b,b/(a+b²)). Its determinant is a+b² and its trace is [b²−a−(a+b²)²]/(a+b²). At a=0.1 it is repelling for 0.419992<b<0.789688. A compact trapping polygon and a small excluded neighborhood of that repeller establish existence of a periodic orbit. This argument alone does not establish uniqueness.

Liénard, van der Pol, and Duffing

For van der Pol, x″+μ(x²−1)x′+x=0, the supplied Liénard hypotheses hold for μ>0: f is even, g=x is odd and restoring, and F=μ(x³/3−x) changes sign once on the positive axis at √3 and then increases without bound. Thus there is a unique stable limit cycle. Weak damping gives an almost circular orbit of amplitude near 2; stronger damping gives slow drifts and fast jumps.

Averaging and the pumped swing

The pendulum frequency correction ω≈1−A²/16 agrees closely with its energy-integral frequency for the tested A=0.1, 0.4, 0.8. Green's theorem applied to a circular approximation of weak van der Pol gives επR²(1−R²/4)=0, selecting R≈2.

The cropped cubic-damping exercise uses x″+εx′³+x=0. Its leading averaged prediction is x≈cos(t)/√(1+3εt/4) for the supplied unit initial displacement. At ε=2 on 0≤t≤50, its maximum position error is 0.253; the test records the visible early discrepancy as well as the later decay.

For x″+(1+εγ+ε cos 2t) sin x=0, exact rest remains rest. A small initial angle of 0.005 grows to a maximum absolute angle of 0.1909 by t=160 for ε=0.1, γ=0; the detuned γ=1 run stays below 0.0086. The leading small-angle instability band is |γ|<1/2. This time-dependent model needs a seed and its averaging approximation has a stated small-angle range.

What connects these tests?

Each system has a state, a rule for changing it, and questions about where it settles. For SIMS, the practical lesson is to measure both agreement and its stability, and to distinguish a changed drawing from changed contacts.

The continuous equations are independent mathematical experiments. A future coupling to SIMS decision rules would need a stated behavioral assumption. Repeated switching could decay, persist, or depend on initial conditions; these are distinct outcomes to measure. The current results concern the models and parameter choices tested here.
