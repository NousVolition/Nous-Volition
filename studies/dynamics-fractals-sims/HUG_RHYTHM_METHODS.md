# Hug rhythm from simulated measurements

This extension tests the physical-unit **synthetic calibration fixture** from batch 13. All recordings remain simulated. The four clay coefficients, effective mass, restoring stiffness and two feedback coefficients fitted earlier stay fixed. The 45.95-second example in batch 12 used different chosen mechanical scaling; it is not the expected period of this calibration fixture or of an unmeasured physical Hug.

[Protocol](hug_rhythm_protocol.json) · [Results and simulated readings](hug_rhythm_results.json) · [Tests](test_hug_rhythm.py) · [Illustrated report](report.html#hug-rhythm).

## Recorded quantities and the model

The model is the same one-mode Burgers clay/active-feedback reduction used by the calibration tool:

```text
sigma = G_M (q/L - u - w)
m q_ddot + k q + (V/L) sigma = F_orbit + (a - b q²) q_dot
u_dot = (sigma - G_K u)/eta_K
w_dot = sigma/eta_M
```

The saved synthetic fixture supplies G_M=28,000 Pa, eta_M=5,200,000 Pa·s, G_K=19,000 Pa and eta_K=900,000 Pa·s. Effective mass is 0.2 kg, parallel stiffness 0.7 N/m, feedback gain 0.03 N·s/m and saturation 3,000,000 N·s/m³. The effective length is 0.1 m and sheared volume 1e-7 m³. These are computational assumptions. The controller delay is zero in this fixture; nonzero-delay behaviour is outside this experiment.

The generator uses those known synthetic values. Independent forward predictions use the previously recovered fit values without refitting on rhythm recordings. The calibration input files and pinned orbital eccentricity file are recorded by SHA-256. Fitting and rhythm recordings are separate. This is a test with data generated by the same model family, not validation against an unknown physical mechanism.

The primary autonomous and orbital trajectories run for 1,800 simulation seconds. The start with nonzero clay memory, the disturbed-cycle return and the passive control require longer settlement checks: their initial 1,800-second errors missed the unchanged 1e-4 gate, so these three controls were extended to 4,200 seconds. The original errors remain in `initial_horizon_audit`; visible repetition was insufficient to claim that memory had settled. The final 300 seconds of each observed case are sampled every 0.05 seconds. Gaussian position-measurement noise of 0.0001 and 0.0005 mm is added afterward with three fixed seeds at each level. This noise represents a selected simulated sensor; it is not the resolution of a kitchen ruler. It does not perturb the dynamics, force or clock. Existing calibration fits came from noiseless synthetic data; this extension tests noisy rhythm measurement, not noisy parameter estimation.

Upward zero crossings use hysteresis: the signal must first drop below minus 20% of its observed half-range before another positive crossing is counted. A signal below five times the declared noise level is not assigned a rhythm. The period is the mean interval between crossings; half-amplitude is the mean of half the peak-to-peak range within complete crossing intervals. The stored metrics include interval variability, amplitude variability and early/late drift within the measurement window. Six chosen noise realizations give a sensitivity range, not a confidence interval. Saved observations retain nine decimal places in millimetres; gate calculations use full precision before saving.

## No-orbit checks

Three initial states include small, larger and oppositely directed initial motion, with one nonzero clay-memory state. The late positive-crossing states are compared in **all four variables**, not just displacement. A full-state periodic shooting calculation solves for the period and a section state; an independently integrated variational equation gives Floquet multipliers. One neutral multiplier is expected from the arbitrary cycle phase; transverse multipliers below one establish local attraction of this model's computed cycle.

A state perturbation changes velocity and both internal clay strains. Its return is measured at matching positive-crossing phase, avoiding an incorrect same-clock comparison after the perturbation shifts phase. A separate feedback-off control checks decay. Exactly zero initial state remains zero in the deterministic unforced model; the tests use nonzero starts to study the attracting cycle.

Mechanical and clay energy are tracked as:

```text
E = m q_dot²/2 + k q²/2 + V sigma²/(2 G_M) + V G_K u²/2
E_dot = (a-b q²) q_dot² + F_orbit q_dot
        - V[(sigma-G_K u)²/eta_K + sigma²/eta_M]
```

Integrated work and dissipation do not feed back into the motion. Independent quadrature checks that feedback supplies the cycle's losses. This distinguishes an actively maintained rhythm from a conservative orbit or a decaying transient.

## Circular and eccentric cases

Circular forcing and the six saved reference eccentricities are included: Pasiphae 0.412, Elara 0.212, Himalia 0.160, Europa 0.009, Callisto 0.007 and Thebe 0.018. Mean elements were pinned in the earlier [orbital protocol](orbit_protocol.json); these are not current positions. Only eccentricity is transferred to this reduced forcing comparison.

The signed input is proportional to `(a_orbit/r)^3 sin(2 true_anomaly)` in the fixed body-frame assumption from batch 12. Each waveform is normalized by its time RMS to 6e-6 N. The primary orbital period is **5.7 simulation seconds**, deliberately near twice this fixture's unforced period. Circular and high-eccentricity controls use **4 seconds** to test detuning. These are chosen laboratory/model times and forces; no gravitational coupling or actual lunar period has been calibrated.

Circular forcing repeats twice per full orbit. Nonzero eccentricity generally breaks that half-orbit symmetry. We sample all four simulated state variables at a fixed orbital phase for the last 40 full orbits. For candidate repeats of one to four full orbits, the maximum component difference across the final 20 pairs must be below 1e-4 in the declared scaled state coordinates. The reported repeat concerns the **full simulated state**, which is available in a simulation; position readings alone cannot establish hidden clay-memory repetition. Interval variability can persist within an orbit-periodic response when successive motions within the orbit differ.

No repeat over one to four orbits is reported as an unresolved short-period repeat, not as chaos or proof of quasiperiodicity. Equality of mean periods alone is insufficient for synchronization. The detuned controls show the limits of a near-resonant comparison rather than a general phase-locking map.

## Verification and reproduction

State scaling is q/Q, q_dot/Q, u/(Q/L), w/(Q/L), with Q=0.0001 m and time in seconds. The main DOP853 solutions use the protocol tolerances. For each of the 12 observed cases, a separate unscaled physical-unit Radau calculation starts from the same late state and covers 12 seconds. Tests also compare the scaled equations with the calibration mechanics, check the analytic Jacobian by finite differences, verify the energy identity, and recover a known sinusoid's period. All declared gates, including failed ones if any, remain in the results file.

```sh
python hug_rhythm.py
python hug_rhythm.py --check
python -m unittest test_hug_rhythm -v
python hug_rhythm_report.py
```

The first command reproduces the full trajectories. `--check` verifies saved numerical gates only. The tests do not rerun the long experiment; they combine independent short controls and checks on the saved outcomes. No additional SIMS participant runs or physical observations are introduced.

For physical clay work, start with [the separate compression observation guide](CLAY_STARTER_TEST.md). Those vertical-load observations are useful preliminary evidence, but cannot be pasted into a shear-calibration file as though they measured the same deformation.

The original nonlinear-oscillation interpretation follows [the van der Pol model](https://www.scholarpedia.org/article/Van_der_Pol_model), with full-state periodic-orbit stability understood through [periodic orbit and Floquet theory](https://www.scholarpedia.org/article/Periodic_orbit). These sources do not supply measured dynamics for this Hug.
