Measurement rows

Paste comma-separated or tab-separated rows with the six headers below, or import a sensor export with those headers. Blank cells remain unknown.

force_N: measured external force, excluding actuator and orbit contributions. active_force_N: actual actuator force, not a controller command. orbit_force_N: actual applied orbital waveform force. Position and velocity use the same signed reference direction. At least 12 rows are required; resolving the fastest motion and delay normally needs many more.

What to record in each test
  1. Clay: several load/hold/recovery histories. Fit on some histories; reserve a different load or hold for prediction.
  2. Passive Hug: actuator and orbit off. Fit mass and restoring stiffness on releases; reserve a different release.
  3. Feedback: measure force, position, velocity and delay over different amplitudes. Test the fitted force law on another recording.
  4. Autonomous: release with no continuing external or orbit force. Repeat different starts; record a separate disturbance and subsequent recovery.
  5. Orbit: keep fitted parameters fixed. Record circular and eccentric applied forces separately, including amplitude, period and phase in the notes.

Read the full calibration procedure and limits. Empty templates never count as a passed test.

After exporting

Give the exported JSON file to this chat for analysis, or run python hug_calibration.py PATH_TO_MEASUREMENTS.json --output PATH_TO_PRIVATE_RESULTS from the study folder. The tool fits only fit-labelled recordings and writes parameter estimates and held-out errors to your chosen results folder.

Simulated fixtures verify the software. A physical calibration needs real recordings; passing a prediction test supports the tested motion and loads and does not by itself prove a physical limit cycle.
