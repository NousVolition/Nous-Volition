# First clay test with your ruler, weights, scale and phone

This is a preliminary **compression-and-recovery observation**. It can show slow deformation and recovery of the clay you have. It does not measure the sideways shear coefficients used in the current Hug calibration, and passive clay alone does not establish an active, self-sustained rhythm.

Use the [empty compression record](calibration/compression_observations.csv). Keep your completed record and video outside this published study folder. Do not import these vertical-compression readings into the [shear-calibration form](calibration/measurements.html).

1. Note the clay brand/type and whether it is water-based, air-drying, oil-based or polymer clay. Make a short, flat-topped cylinder. Measure its initial height and diameter and photograph it beside the ruler. Note sample preparation and room conditions; do not add water partway through a trial.
2. Use a light, rigid, flat plate that covers the top. Weigh the plate and the added weights. Record **all mass supported by the sample**, including the plate. A plate left on the sample still applies a load during recovery.
3. Fix the phone side-on, with the ruler in the same plane as the sample's height measurement. Keep the camera still and record continuously. Track the bottom of the plate or the clay's upper edge against a fixed base. Record before loading as well as after unloading.
4. Gently apply the smallest useful load. As an exploratory starting schedule, hold the load for **2 minutes**, then remove it and observe recovery for **5 minutes**. These are chosen pilot observation times, not a standardized material test or a guarantee that recovery finishes in five minutes. Record the actual load/unload times, including the short ramps while adding or removing weights.
5. Read heights from the video at approximately 0, 5, 15, 30, 60 and 120 seconds after loading and again during recovery. Retain the full video so faster changes can be examined later. Do not report finer precision than the ruler and image can resolve. If the motion is too small to resolve, record that limitation instead of guessing.
6. Repeat with a fresh sample of similar dimensions and preparation. Record any plate tilt, slip, cracks, spreading or collapse. Large shape changes and friction under the plate complicate conversion to a material law; keep those observations without treating them as a small-strain calibration.

For a static mass load, the approximate downward force is `force_N = total_supported_mass_g × 0.00980665`. During placement/removal the instantaneous force may differ, so do not assume an abrupt ideal step. Use `height_mm` for the observed height. Height change is `initial_height_mm - height_mm`; retain both the original height and the readings. If the plate stays during recovery, record its remaining mass rather than entering zero force.

For a later quantitative shear test, a fixture must keep the thickness known while one boundary moves sideways under a measured lateral force, with slip controlled. The shear definitions are force divided by sheared area and lateral movement divided by gap thickness. A ruler/phone pilot may not resolve the small motions used in the present synthetic examples. See the [two-plate shear measurement explanation](https://wiki.anton-paar.com/en/basics-of-rheology/) and the [clay creep/recovery source](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2011GL050186).
