    Decision rules
    Copy: random neighbor, 15% refusal of a different choice; optional 2% flip. Hold: original starting choice. Majority / oppose: local majority / opposite; retain current choice on ties.
    Evidence
    Predictions recorded before actions; likelihoods scored on observed context and outcomes. Four rules, each starting at 25% support. After each action, multiply each weight by its predicted probability of that action, then normalize the four weights to total 100%. Actual rule settings excluded from investigator scoring.
    Response trigger
    At least 12 actions, plus either an impossible action under the claim or a unique alternative with ≥80% relative support and ≥10× the claim’s likelihood. Revising a claim to another rule also requires that alternative to be identified. A contradicted claim can trigger behavior alignment even when the alternatives remain tied.
    Responses
    Editable, deterministic participant policies. Dialogue is generated from evidence and policy; the investigator is named Feynman in this model.
    Rounds
    Round 1 uses the starting evidence. Its follow-up becomes round 2’s evidence, and so on. Claims, behavior rules, network choices, and promise history carry forward. Decisions pause during exchanges.
    Fresh evidence
    Each action block starts with equal likelihood weights; old and new behavior are scored separately. Network: 120 seconds, 25% activation, shuffled turns. Probes: fixed own and neighbor choices, 8 repetitions per context. Hold always refers to the original starting choice.
    Promise only
    P means the SIM promised a rule different from its current behavior but did not change its behavior rule. This is a simulator fact. Feynman separately evaluates fresh actions as supporting the promise, favoring an alternative, contradicting it, or inconclusive.
    History
    C and B mark changes from the previous round, including reversals. A dash means no rule change or promise to change an unaligned behavior occurred. Earlier promises and their follow-up evidence are included in the next exchange. Response policies are editable assumptions.
