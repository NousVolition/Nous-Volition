When substances meet

NousVolition / My-Sources-Project-ALL · Reproducible multiphase interaction pilot · 10 October 2026 UTC

Mixing, dissolution, surface attachment, reaction and electrostatic motion are different processes. This study runs separate models to identify which process is present, with explicit controls and limits.

Completed: 21 gas/liquid partition runs, two carbonate equilibrium solves, a CO–OH chemical kinetic benchmark with oxidant/rate sensitivities, 2D transport and refinement, 7,680 independent Brownian charged/neutral pair trajectories, 28 vapor/interface cases, nine acetone–ice adsorption runs, and analytic scavenging, coagulation and freezing/melting comparisons. All 27 verification tests passed; the delivered test log records the completed run.

Evidence level: published constants plus numerical verification against analytic limits. There is no new laboratory dataset, field calibration, or validation of a complete smoke/fog environment. Calculations here are not exposure predictions. No physical generation of CO, smoke or VOC vapors was performed or proposed.

Transport is distinct from chemical identity

The 2D equation is ∂c/∂t + ∇·(u c) = D∇²c − λc. In the inert test λ = 0, D = 2×10⁻⁵ m²/s and u = (0.01, 0.003) m/s. These are specified benchmark inputs, not measured CO diffusion or room ventilation data. A sinusoidal initial field provides an exact Fourier solution, making numerical error measurable. The grid study reduced relative L² error from 3.417% at 24² cells to 0.473% at 192² cells. First-order upwind advection causes numerical diffusion; remaining spatial error is reported, not hidden.

Gas-only CO controls at 0%, 50% and 100% relative humidity use identical prescribed D and flow and therefore give identical transport. This demonstrates absence of an inserted CO–vapor reaction; it does not establish that humidity never changes real transport coefficients. Uniform gas buoyancy, temperature evolution, turbulence and Navier–Stokes momentum are not solved. Gravity can move droplets relative to air without molecular repulsion. All particle-pair plots use a frame moving with their common settling velocity.

A smoke surrogate is a 100 nm radius inert spherical aerosol population, not smoke chemistry. A diffusion-only Smoluchowski collection model with 10 µm droplets, 0.5 g/m³ water and unit sticking captures 0.2020% of particle material in 600 s. This is a restricted mechanism estimate. Real activation, impaction, phoresis, hygroscopicity and surface composition can dominate. A captured-material reservoir accompanies the 2D sink. Measured collection-efficiency study, field fog scavenging study.

Like and opposite cases use charges +1000e/+1000e and +1000e/−1000e. The neutral case has zero charge on both droplets. Each mean includes 1,280 independent pairs across five recorded seeds. Opposite charge attracts, like charge repels, and the neutral ensemble has no imposed Coulomb drift. A small increase in mean neutral radial distance is compatible with Brownian spreading and the geometry of measuring distance; it is not evidence of repulsion.

The overdamped equation is dxᵢ = bᵢFᵢdt + √(2Dᵢ) dWᵢ, with b = Cc/(6πµa), D = kBTb, and Coulomb force F = kₑq₁q₂r/r³. For equal mobilities and no noise, r³(t) = r₀³ + 6b kₑq₁q₂t. Deterministic timestep refinement matches this limit. A separate Brownian mean-square-displacement test checks ⟨|Δx|²⟩ = 6Dt. Slip coefficients follow the NIST aerosol reference.

These are independent pairs, not a collective many-particle charged cloud. Simulations stop a pair when its center separation reaches six radii. That stop records entry into an unmodeled near-field region; it is not a collision, sticking event or coalescence. Fixed charges, rigid spherical geometry, constant radius and independent drag omit polarization, hydrodynamic interactions and lubrication. A charged particle can attract a neutral polarizable object even though q₁q₂ = 0; the monopole control cannot predict that attraction. The aerosol charge/size sensitivity results that reach this boundary are censored and should not be interpreted as final contact distances.

Electron-cloud overlap produces short-range quantum repulsion between molecules at close contact. Attractive dispersion, electrostatics and bonding also contribute at molecular scales. None of that means ordinary neutral gases refuse to mix. Droplet-scale Coulomb separation is a different, explicitly modeled force. Buoyancy, drag, settling and limited solubility can create spatial separation without a repulsive pair force.

Water phase changes and ice surfaces

At −10 °C, saturation over ice is 259.89 Pa, below 286.45 Pa over supercooled liquid water. A parcel initially at 95% RH relative to liquid is supersaturated relative to ice. Thus the separate thermostatted tests deposit vapor on ice while evaporating supercooled liquid. Water remains H₂O throughout. The interface model uses 1 L gas, 0.01 m² area, 0.001 m/s transfer velocity and a 10 g condensed reservoir. Water inventory is conserved; latent heat is recorded as thermostat heat, not ignored or used to predict free temperature evolution. Murphy and Koop (2005).

A separate seeded melting/freezing energy balance at 273.15 K converts 8.982 g of a 10 g inventory in 600 s under ±5 W. It uses a rounded 334 kJ/kg heat of fusion. It does not predict spontaneous freezing, nucleation delays, crystal habit or riming probability.

At 210 K, the two-site published acetone–ice adsorption model puts 59.196% of a closed 1 ppb acetone inventory onto an assumed 1 m² ice surface by 600 s. This depends strongly on surface area and site density. Site 1 uses the reported capacity upper bound, and gas supply is assumed perfectly mixed without a boundary-layer resistance. The source describes preparation/aging effects and fits only 190–220 K. This result is a conditional surface-kinetics calculation, not a prediction for ordinary frost at room pressure. Published two-site adsorption model.

Equations, assumptions and verification

Compartment partitioning: ṅl = kLa Vl(Hcp RT ng/Vg − nl/Vl), ṅg = −ṅl. The independent analytic equilibrium is fl = Hcp RT Vl/(Vg + Hcp RT Vl). The relaxation rate is kLa(1 + Hcp RT Vl/Vg). Bulk kLa = 0.01 s⁻¹ is assumed. For droplets kL = Dl/a, kg = Dg/a, KL = (1/kL + Hcp RT/kg)⁻¹ and kLa = 3KL/a, with Dl = 2×10⁻⁹ and Dg = 2×10⁻⁵ m²/s as engineering inputs. This is a two-film closure, not a resolved liquid diffusion field. No accommodation resistance or curvature correction is fitted.

Chemical kinetics: the CO–OH benchmark explicitly accounts for CO, OH, CO₂ and H. It uses finite oxidant, an evaluated rate and separate C/O/H balances. At 100 Pa the evaluated combined-rate pressure correction is below 0.06%; that small correction and stabilization branch are omitted as a stated low-pressure approximation. Radical chain closure, wall chemistry and external OH production are absent. No rate for CO + water is invented.

Numerics: adaptive Radau or Runge–Kutta integration for the small compartments, a scalar root solve for carbonate, conservative periodic flux differences with SSPRK3 for transport, and Euler–Maruyama for Brownian particle pairs. The transport timestep is checked against a positive forward-Euler bound. Gas/liquid inventory, carbonate carbon, kinetic C/O/H, water phase inventory and captured particle material are tracked. Total chemical-species moles are not assumed conserved across arbitrary reactions: the H₂O₂ decomposition audit conserves atoms while producing O₂.

Refinement and controls: 24², 48², 96² and 192² transport grids; fixed-grid time refinement; charge drift timestep refinement; stochastic timestep distributions; zero flow/diffusion, no uptake, no sticking, no oxidant and no-charge controls; fixed seeds; Brownian Einstein and Coulomb/Stokes checks; carbonate electroneutrality; pure-water and saturation-reference limits. Agreement with these benchmarks verifies implementation of the reduced equations. It does not validate all omitted physics.

The largest reported relative inventory residual across the completed budgets is 2.48e-15. Some residuals are small by construction because complementary reservoirs are used; independent analytic solutions, charge balance and physical invariants provide additional checks. Unit test output is saved in test_log.txt.

Sensitivity: water partitioning spans 278.15–308.15 K, water loading 0.1–1 g/m³ and droplet radii 5–25 µm. Gas-only RH controls span 0–100%; phase exchange spans RHwater 50–105% and −15 to 25 °C. Flow spans 0–0.03 m/s. Charge simulations vary 0–3000 elementary charges, 5–20 µm radius and temperature. Smaller-particle scavenging spans 50 nm–1 µm radius. Five repeated seeds distinguish Brownian variability from deterministic drift. Isoprene Henry values span the retrieved literature spread 1.3×10⁻⁴ to 3.4×10⁻⁴ mol/(m³ Pa).

The 4,000-draw-per-solute uncertainty exercise uses selected distributions: lognormal Henry variability (geometric SD 1.3, except uniform literature range for isoprene), uniform water loading and radius, and lognormal gas/liquid diffusivity variability (geometric SD 2). Reported 5th–95th percentiles are assumption-driven ranges, not measured confidence intervals. Temperature sweeps use local van’t Hoff slopes; they are not an uncertainty model. CO–OH rate multipliers use the source’s ±0.05 log10 reliability range. No equivalent rate distribution is invented for unspecified smoke adsorption or catalysis.

Scope limits: no molecular-dynamics or electronic-structure calculation, no universal smoke chemistry, no VOC oxidation network, no live CFD pressure/velocity/buoyancy solve, no evolving droplet population coupled to evaporation, and no experimental exposure or smell test. Neutral molecules mix through thermal motion and collisions. Identity, intermolecular affinity, phase availability, charge, size, surface composition and environment control outcomes more directly than molecular mass alone. Navier–Stokes remains a continuum momentum equation; reactive transport and molecular chemistry supply additional equations and constitutive data. This study proposes no new Navier–Stokes law.

Data provenance

Constants were transcribed from the following public references. Accessed 10 October 2026 UTC. Scenario choices are marked as assumptions; they are not disguised as measured inputs.

  • Sander2023: Sander (2023), Compilation of Henry's law constants, version 5.0.0 — Hcp definition and 298.15 K values; temperature slopes are local van't Hoff fits, not global phase-equilibrium laws.
  • CO_H: https://henrys-law.org/henry/casrn/630-08-0 — Burkholder 2019 review; consistent with Rettich et al. 1982 measurements, DOI 10.1002/BBPC.198200051
  • CO2_H: https://henrys-law.org/henry/casrn/124-38-9 — Burkholder 2019 review; freshwater, not seawater
  • acetone_H: https://henrys-law.org/henry/casrn/67-64-1 — Burkholder 2019 review; dilute solute
  • isoprene_H: https://henrys-law.org/henry/casrn/78-79-5 — Plyasunov and Shock 2000 literature value. Recent measured entries reach 3.4e-4; this spread is explicitly explored.
  • H2O2_H: https://henrys-law.org/henry/casrn/7722-84-1 — Burkholder 2019 review; neutral H2O2 in dilute water
  • N2_H: https://henrys-law.org/henry/casrn/7727-37-9 — 298.15 K database value; temperature slope not used in headline results
  • O2_H: https://henrys-law.org/henry/casrn/7782-44-7 — 298.15 K database value; temperature slope not used in headline results
  • USGS2012: USGS technical memorandum 2012.05, carbonate speciation — At 25 C, dilute freshwater: Kw=1e-14, K1=10^-6.35, K2=10^-10.33. CO2* apparent acid convention. No salinity/activity corrections.
  • MurphyKoop2005: Murphy and Koop, QJRMS 131, 1539-1565 (2005) — Equations 7 (ice) and 10 (liquid water) vapor pressure in Pa with T in K.
  • NIST2006: Mulholland et al. (2006), NIST aerosol size measurements — Section 3.3 eq18 Kim slip coefficients 1.165, 0.483, 0.997; mean free path 67.3 nm at 296.15K, 101.325kPa; Sutherland scaling.
  • IceAcetone2006: Acetone Adsorption on Ice Surfaces in the Temperature Range T=190-220K: Evidence for Aging Effects Due to Crystallographic Changes of the Adsorption Sites — Two-site adsorption/desorption rate fits and capacities from abstract. k_ads1=3.8e-14 sqrt(T), k_des1=4e11 exp(-5773/T); k_ads2=2.9e-15 sqrt(T), k_des2=1.5e7 exp(-3488/T). k_ads in cm3 molecule^-1 s^-1, k_des in s^-1. Capacity1 <=1e14 cm^-2, capacity2=6e14 cm^-2. Assume the upper bound for site1. Kinetic ratios used consistently; separately fitted equilibrium parameters not substituted.
  • Depee2021: Depee et al. (2021), Laboratory study of collection efficiency of submicron aerosol particles by cloud droplets, Part I — Evidence that humidity, phoresis, particle/drop size and collection efficiency matter. Our simplified diffusion kernel is not fitted or validated against these measurements.
  • Gilardoni2014: Gilardoni et al. (2014), Fog scavenging of organic and inorganic aerosol in the Po Valley — Smoke/aerosol composition and hygroscopicity matter; activation and impaction cannot be inferred from particle mass alone.
  • Do2009: Do et al. (2009), Hydrogen peroxide decomposition on manganese oxide (pyrolusite): kinetics, intermediates, and mechanism — pH7 catalytic decomposition, observed maximum kobs=.741 min^-1 at H2O2/surface-site ratio11.8. Full temperature/loading specification not recovered; NOT applied to clean water or generic smoke and NOT simulated.
  • IUPAC_CO_OH: IUPAC datasheet HOx_VOC10, evaluation updated 2005 — Evaluated combined k=1.44e-13(1+[N2]/4.2e19) cm3 molecule^-1 s^-1 over200-300K, 0-1bar N2. Low-pressure channel CO+OH->CO2+H simulated at298.15K,100Pa inN2 with k0=1.44e-13; combined pressure correction <0.06%, neglected HOCO stabilization. Finite OH inventory. No rate borrowed for CO+water. Quoted log10(k) reliability +/-0.05 used in sensitivity, not a statistical confidence interval.
  • Crowley2010: Crowley et al. (2010), Evaluated heterogeneous chemistry on solid substrates — Ice surface chemistry requires species- and temperature-specific uptake data; liquid Henry constants cannot be used as ice adsorption constants.
