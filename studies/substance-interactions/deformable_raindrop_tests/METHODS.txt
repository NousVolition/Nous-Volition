DEFORMABLE LARGE-RAINDROP MODEL: WHAT IS ACTUALLY SOLVED

INTERIM RELEASE, 20 OCTOBER 2026: 24 of 28 planned airflow cases are complete.
The long-averaging control was interrupted; two interpolation checks and
the Re=2600, 36-cell refinement were not run. Statements below describing
the numerical design do not imply those four checks passed. Consult
study_status.json for completed versus unfinished work. Physical source
files and coefficients remain unchanged from the pre-extraction freeze.

This extends the fixed-body pilot with a volume-preserving liquid shape model
and a refined three-dimensional gas calculation. It is a reduced model, not a
fully resolved two-phase free-surface simulation. One axisymmetric spheroidal
coordinate is allowed to move. Neither its equilibrium value nor its motion
is prescribed from an experimental shape correlation.

LIQUID SHAPE, SI UNITS
Let D be volume-equivalent diameter, R=D/2, s=log(aspect),
a=R exp(2s/3), b=R exp(-s/3). The flow-direction semiaxis is a; the two
transverse semiaxes are b. V=4 pi a b^2/3=pi D^3/6 at every time.
The affine liquid velocity is (2 sdot x/3, -sdot y/3, -sdot z/3).
It is divergence free, irrotational, and follows the spheroidal surface.
Integrating its kinetic energy over a uniform-density ellipsoid gives
T=0.5 M(s) sdot^2, M=m(4a^2+2b^2)/45, with m=rho_water V.
Its viscous dissipation rate is (4/3) mu_water V sdot^2.
The exact spheroid area is evaluated by 64-point Gauss-Legendre quadrature:
S=2 pi b integral[-1,1] sqrt(a^2(1-u^2)+b^2 u^2) du.
The surface-energy derivative is analytic under that integral, rather than a
finite-difference force. The motion obeys
  M sddot + 0.5 Mprime sdot^2 + (4/3) mu_water V sdot + sigma Sprime = Q_air.
This is a variational, one-coordinate approximation to liquid dynamics.
It does not solve a general liquid pressure/velocity field or enforce every
pointwise tangential-stress condition. Air added mass is neglected.

At small amplitude around a sphere, the model gives the Rayleigh quadrupole
frequency omega^2=8 sigma/(rho_water R^3) and Lamb amplitude decay rate
beta=5 mu_water/(rho_water R^2). These are independently verified limits,
not experimental validation of finite deformation. A sphere's static Laplace
pressure 2 sigma/R follows from d(sigma S)/dV; no two-fluid pressure jump is
discretized here, so a free-interface Laplace-pressure solver test is not
claimed. All gravity work associated with shape alone vanishes because the
center and volume are fixed. Weight minus air buoyancy enters translation.

AIRFLOW AND CURVED SURFACE
The gas solves the 3D D3Q19 regularized isothermal lattice-Boltzmann equations
with a fixed Smagorinsky coefficient Cs=0.1 and air Reynolds number Re=UD/nu.
The nominal rectangular domain is 12D long and 5D in each transverse
direction. The body center is 3D downstream. An equilibrium inlet and a
copied-population outlet are used, with periodic transverse boundaries.
These are finite-domain approximations; a wider/longer control is included.
Each gas solve holds one geometry fixed. The interpolation library supplies
the response to changing geometry in the separate liquid equation.

The surface is defined analytically. The exact intersection of each lattice
link with the spheroid determines the linear interpolated bounce-back
distance (Bouzidi et al.). Thus the effective wall is not simply halfway
between staircase nodes. The interface is still treated as no-slip and
immobile during each gas calculation. No internal liquid circulation,
time-dependent interface velocity, clean-surface tangential mobility or
Marangoni stress is communicated to this airflow solver.

Gas drag is the boundary momentum exchange projected along the flow. The
deformation load Q is its virtual work against dr/ds=(2x/3,-y/3,-z/3), using
the exact intersection position relative to the center. An isotropic
reference-pressure contribution is removed. Finite links have a nonzero
discrete constant-pressure moment, so its contribution at the boundary's
weighted mean pressure is removed too. The uncorrected load and correction
are retained. A manufactured P2 pressure field supplies an independent
analytic surface-work check; its measured discretization error is reported.
Generalized load units are N m; Cq=Q/(rho_air U^2 D^3). Drag uses
Cd=F/(0.5 rho_air U^2 pi b^2), with projected area changing with aspect.

Regularized collision conserves local mass to floating-point precision by
assigning the rounding residual to the rest population. Interpolated wall
reflection is NOT exactly mass conservative. A separate ledger explicitly
quantifies that unphysical leakage and the inlet/outlet transfer; it does
not pretend leakage is physical. Production runs store total mass offsets,
not a full per-step integrated flux ledger. Open-domain total mass offsets
alone are not a conservation residual. Density and speed guards stop
unstable calculations; unsuccessful runs must not enter a prediction table.

COUPLING AND PREDICTIONS
A rectangular table in Re and aspect is interpolated linearly. No
extrapolation beyond the computed range is permitted. Terminal speed and
equilibrium shape are solved together from drag=weight-buoyancy and
sigma Sprime=Q_air. Terminal speeds are predictions, not supplied measured
speeds. Only measured shape outcomes are scored in this new comparison.

Eliminating rho_air U^2 between the two balances shows why size and surface
tension dominate these nominal equilibrium-shape sensitivities. If Sbar=S/D^2
and Bo_D=(rho_water-rho_air) g D^2/sigma, then
  dSbar/ds = (4/3) Bo_D exp(2s/3) Cq/Cd.
Air density still affects buoyancy, the solved speed and Re-dependent loads;
it is not exactly irrelevant. This identity is a consequence of this model's
force balances, not a new fluid law or an empirical fit. It does not account
for the mean-shape shifts produced by missing transverse oscillations.

The transient examples perturb the predicted equilibrium shape, then evolve
the liquid at its predicted fixed terminal speed. Shape changes feed back
through the load table; the wake adjusts instantaneously in this
quasi-steady approximation. The mean load has no shape-rate dependence, so
aerodynamic damping and wake-driven sustained excitation are absent. The
gas and liquid are not advanced in a single
moving-boundary calculation. Translation is not time-integrated in these
examples. There is no prescribed sinusoidal shape, breakup, coalescence,
evaporation, electrical charge, aerosol collection or smoke retention.

The liquid energy ledger integrates aerodynamic work and viscous loss:
E(t)-E(0)-integral Q sdot dt+integral C sdot^2 dt=0.
It checks energy within the reduced subsystem, not a global two-phase
energy balance. The gas is isothermal and maintained by an external inlet.

UNCERTAINTY AND RESOLUTION
Different cells-per-diameter, lattice speeds, domain sizes, seeds and
averaging windows diagnose numerical sensitivity. Four time-block means
describe variation in each force record. They are not guaranteed independent
and are not presented as a statistical confidence interval. Repeated seeds
diagnose wake sensitivity, not independent experimental replication.
One-at-a-time property, size and load perturbations are scenario ranges,
not a calibrated joint uncertainty distribution. Geometry/Re table density
can cause interpolation error beyond the grid controls. Boundary-layer and
wake resolution remains limited; a stable calculation is not a DNS claim.

EXTERNAL MEASUREMENT POLICY
The new measurement set is Andsager, Beard & Laird (1999), Table2, all four
25.5m rows. Source and model selection preceded numerical table extraction;
previews had exposed qualitative outcomes. No parameters were fitted.
An equilibrium spheroid is compared with a measured time-mean chord ratio.
That observable mismatch, missing transverse oscillations and unmatched
individual experimental temperatures prevent a fully matched validation.
The selected table has all uncertainties and counts in measurements.csv.
See source_audit.json for the preserved Table2/Table3 4mm discrepancy.
