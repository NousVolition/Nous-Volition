Five equations, tested together

Supplement to the NousVolition / My-Sources-Project-ALL study

Yes: all five can be tested together when their connection is defined. Completed: 20 mathematical checks, 8 coupled-model checks, and 12 transport runs. All 28 checks passed. The shared simulation uses the five equations to control an imposed flow carrying CO, acetone and hydrogen peroxide through fog.

This is an explicitly constructed numerical benchmark. The chosen flow connections have not been measured in a real apparatus. They test whether the equations and transport accounting work consistently together; they do not establish that clock, circuit or oscillator equations determine molecular reactions. Images 4 and 5 are cropped; their equations are reconstructed from the visible terms and diagram.

The completed combined test

The domain is a periodic 1 m × 1 m layer of depth 1 m, at 25°C and 101,325 Pa. It contains a fixed, uniformly distributed fog-water volume of 0.5 mL (about 0.5 g) per cubic metre of air, with 10 µm droplet radius and imposed 100% relative humidity. Initial mean wet-gas concentrations are CO 1 ppm, acetone 1 ppm and H₂O₂ 0.001 ppm. Each initially has the spatial profile C/C₀=1+½cos(2πx/L)cos(2πy/L). Liquid starts solute-free. The simulation lasts 180 seconds.

The carrier velocity is uₓ=Aₓ(t)sin(2πy/L), uᵧ=Aᵧ(t)sin(2πx/L). Its divergence is zero. Every screenshot contributes an explicitly bounded signal to the amplitudes:

Aₓ=0.01[1+0.2hcycle+0.2hclock] m/s
Aᵧ=0.01[0.6+0.2hjunction+0.1hoscillator+0.1hgate] m/s

What supplies the substance physics

Define G as gas solute moles per gas volume, and Q as fog-liquid solute moles per gas volume. Actual liquid concentration is Q/ε with ε=5×10⁻⁷. For each solute m=HcpRT. The two-phase equations are

∂G/∂t + ∇·(uG) = Dg∇²G − J
∂Q/∂t + ∇·(uQ) = J
J = kLa (εmG − Q).

Local exchange is integrated exactly and conserves G+Q. Its equilibrium aqueous fraction is εm/(1+εm). Gas diffusivity Dg=2×10⁻⁵ m²/s and liquid diffusivity Dl=2×10⁻⁹ m²/s are shared illustrative values, not separately fitted species measurements. For droplet radius a=10⁻⁵ m, kl=Dl/a, kg=Dg/a, KL=[1/kl+m/kg]−1, and the liquid-volume transfer coefficient kLa=3KL/a, in s⁻¹. Here the conventional symbol kLa denotes a volumetric coefficient; it is not kl times the droplet radius.

The literature Henry coefficients at 298.15 K are Hcp=9.7×10⁻⁶ for CO, 0.27 for acetone, and 860 for H₂O₂, all in mol m⁻³ Pa⁻¹. Source: Sander’s 2023 Henry-law compilation, selected review entries documented in references.json. These encode species-specific partitioning; molecular mass alone does not set the answer.

The transfer model contains no chemical reaction term. “Zero conversion” is therefore an explicit control assumption, not a new experimental finding that reactions can never occur. There is no OH radical, light-driven chemistry or catalyst here. In particular, mixing CO with water does not get an invented CO→CO₂ term. CO₂ is a different species, and its dissolution and acid–base equilibrium remain in the broader substance study. H₂O₂ is used here solely as a soluble tracer; catalytic decomposition needs a specified catalyst and validated kinetics.

Fog liquid co-moves horizontally with the air; settling, evaporation, droplet growth, heat balance and solute feedback are excluded. There are no charged particles in this combined supplement, hence no modeled electrical attraction or repulsion. Ice, smoke collection, surface adsorption and Coulomb/Brownian interaction retain their separate models in the broader study. This supplement does not pretend to couple all of them using the five screenshots.

Numerical verification and limits

Transport uses conservative finite-volume MUSCL fluxes with a monotonized-central slope limiter, centered diffusion, a three-stage strong-stability-preserving time integrator, and exact exchange in a split update. Initial conditions are exact cell averages of the same continuous field. The main grid is 48×48 with Δt=0.25 s. An explicit positivity timestep bound is enforced; concentrations stayed nonnegative. The maximum relative species inventory residual across reported cases and grids is 4.44e-16. No chemical mass is silently lost.

Why the five equations cannot simply be identified with one another

They evolve different quantities: radius and angle, superconducting phase and voltage, clock-hand angles, a bifurcation coordinate, and position plus velocity. Equal-looking symbols do not give them equal units or mechanisms. They can coexist in a larger simulation through a specified controller, boundary condition or force law. They cannot replace the missing chemical constants, surface properties or forces just because their plots show circles, attraction or separation. Quantum bonding determines chemical identity; continuum flow and species equations describe transport at much larger scales. No new Navier–Stokes law is proposed.

Independent checks of each screenshot

1. A stable unit-circle orbit, with an exception

θ̇ = 1,   ṙ = (1 − r²)r

For r₀ ≥ 0, a version of the exact solution that also handles the origin is

r(t) = r₀ / √[r₀² + (1 − r₀²)e−2t],   θ(t) = θ₀ + t.

At r₀ = 0 the Cartesian state remains (0,0), and a polar angle is not physically defined there. For every r₀ > 0, the radius tends to 1. At r₀ = 1, it is already a periodic orbit with period 2π. For 0 < r₀ < 1, the path spirals outward; for r₀ > 1, inward. “Every radius approaches 1” needs the r₀ > 0 condition.

The screenshot’s logarithmic integration uses √(1−r²), which is real only inside the circle. The corresponding antiderivative on either side is ln r − ½ ln|1−r²|. Equilibria r=0 and r=1 must be handled separately when variables are separated. The final flow formula is nevertheless valid for all nonnegative radii at forward times.

For r₀>1, backward flow has a finite blow-up boundary. At r₀=2, the real solution requires t>-0.143841036. The origin and unit circle are treated separately in the tests. Radial perturbations near the cycle shrink by e−4π≈3.4873424e-06 per revolution; angular phase offsets do not decay.

2. The Josephson-junction / pendulum analogy works after scaling

C V̇ + V/R + Ic sinφ = I,   V = ℏφ̇/(2e)

Substitution gives (ℏC/2e)φ̈ + (ℏ/2eR)φ̇ + Icsinφ = I. Set ωp=√(2eIc/ℏC), τ=ωpt, α=1/(RCωp), and i=I/Ic:

d²φ/dτ² + α dφ/dτ + sinφ = i.

A pendulum with constant torque has the same normalized equation. Its inertia is mL², not merely m when length varies. This is an analogy of equations after the coefficients and units are matched. A superconducting phase difference is not a literal pendulum angle, and voltage requires the factor ℏ/(2e).

The example uses Ic=1 µA, C=1 pF and α=0.2, which implies R=90.706392 Ω and ωp=5.51229e+10 rad/s. Direct dimensional circuit integration agrees with the normalized equation. At i=0.5 the resting initial condition locks at φ=arcsin(0.5)=π/6. A sufficiently rotating initial condition keeps running at that same bias. This illustrates bistability for these selected underdamped parameters, not universal behavior for every circuit.

3. Clock alignment: 1:05:27.2727…

The minute hand moves at 6°/min and the hour hand at 0.5°/min. Their relative angular speed is 5.5°/min. The next same-direction coincidence satisfies 5.5t=360, so t=720/11 minutes: 65 minutes and 27.2727… seconds after noon. Integration of the two angular motions gives 65.454545454546 minutes, an error of 5.68e-14 minutes.

An alternative derivation sets the minute-hand angle equal to one full turn plus the hour-hand angle: 6t=360+0.5t. “Aligned” here means pointing in the same direction. If merely being on the same straight line, pointing oppositely, also counts, the first such event is 360/11 minutes after noon, at 12:32:43.6363… .

4. Saddle-node scaling: a=½, b=−½

ẋ = r+x²;   x=rau,   t=rbτ.

The visible rescaling yields ra−bdu/dτ = r+r2au². Balancing all exponents requires a−b=1=2a. Thus a=½ and b=−½. For r>0, u=x/√r and τ=√r t reduce the equation to du/dτ=1+u². The scaled trajectories collapse onto u(τ)=tan(τ−arctan2) for the chosen initial u=−2.

The exact transit time from x=−1 to +1 is T(r)=2 arctan(1/√r)/√r. The asymptotic π/√r applies when r is small relative to the squared cutoff. It is not an exact formula for a finite cutoff.

5. Harmonic-oscillator ellipses; a false spiral from Euler

From the visible vector components (v,0) and (0,−ω²x), the fifth image is interpreted as ẋ=v, v̇=−ω²x. Its exact invariant is v²+ω²x². Trajectories are clockwise closed ellipses in the position–velocity plane, with period 2π/ω. Position and velocity are not two spatial coordinates of interacting droplets.

Tests use ω=1, x₀=1, v₀=0 over ten periods. For forward Euler, each step multiplies energy by exactly 1+ω²Δt², so the numerical trajectory spirals outward despite the physical equation conserving energy. Velocity Verlet has bounded energy oscillation and converges at second order; the adaptive high-order solver agrees with the exact sinusoid.

How this relates to the substance study

A stable circle in a state-space plot does not by itself demonstrate molecular attraction or repulsion. Image 1 specifies a vector field; if interpreted as a planar fluid velocity it has divergence 2−4(x²+y²), so it is not an incompressible 2D carrier flow. A compressible interpretation would require its own density and boundary conditions. Image 2 is an equation-level analogy. Image 3 has relative motion without any hand-to-hand force. Image 4 describes a local dynamical bottleneck. Image 5 is a restoring-force oscillator, and its Euler spiral demonstrates why numerical refinement matters.

The combined test above supplies explicit state variables, units, conservation balances and assumed flow connections. None of the screenshots itself supplies a chemical reaction rate or a Coulomb force law. A model intended to predict a particular apparatus would additionally require measured forcing, calibrated coefficients and validation against data.
