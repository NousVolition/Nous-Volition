Purpose and scope

This is an agent-based social analogy using SIMS (simulated participants) to examine mechanism sufficiency and implementation sensitivity. It is not a calibrated account of tribalism. No Hénon dynamics, Navier–Stokes solver, molecular forces, phase transition equation, or physical time unit enters the model. “Freeze,” “melt,” “disperse,” and “condense” describe increasing rigidity, decreasing rigidity, exploring contacts, and affiliation convergence, respectively. They are interpretation labels, not six separate physical states or automatically assigned properties of the six words.

Entities, initialization, and counterbalancing

An agent has a fixed network site, an affiliation g in {0,…,5}, persistent individual behavioral traits, a directed trust vector T, and rigidity r in [0,1]. There are no demographic categories or real-person attributes. All trust and rigidity begin at zero. Each run has 40 baseline, 40 intervention, and 40 recovery rounds; only stress-enabled runs receive the intervention.

Networks have exactly 3N undirected edges, no self-edges, and a connected ring backbone. Ring: degree six everywhere. Random: a degree-two ring plus 2N uniformly chosen missing edges. Hub: a degree-two ring plus 2N missing edges incident to three randomly selected sites. Thus “random” is not an unconstrained Erdős–Rényi graph. Sites are not spatial coordinates; mobility means contact exploration.

Equal groups have N/6 members. Unequal group counts use weights [0.35,0.25,0.15,0.10,0.10,0.05] on N−6 slots, add one guaranteed member to each, then distribute rounding remainders. At N=60, one rotation is [20,15,9,6,6,4]. Sizes rotate across group IDs. Agents are shuffled over network sites. Trait profile = (group + rotation + floor(rotation/6)) mod 6; twelve rotations in each seed split counterbalance labels with trait and size ranks, including every size-rank/trait pairing. This is exact marginal/rank counterbalancing, not exhaustive permutations of all individuals and graphs.

Profiles (holding tendency, exploration probability, generosity offset) are (0.65,0.10,−0.12), (0.50,0.12,0), (0.35,0.16,+0.12), (0.40,0.14,−0.06), (0.30,0.18,+0.06), (0.55,0.13,0). Identical agents use (0.45,0.14,0). Traits stay with an individual after affiliation changes; words never fix these traits.

Per-round process and equations

  1. Candidate menu. Start with network neighbors. With probability exploration × (1−0.7r), add one uniformly drawn nonself contact. Uniformly sample up to six eligible candidates, or two under modeled time pressure. Every agent has at least two eligible partners. This is an option-budget surrogate for time pressure, not an explicit reaction-time model.
  2. Information. A per-agent visibility draw exposes remembered trust with probability 1 normally or 0.30 under limited information. Trust still updates from actual outcomes afterward. No misinformation is simulated.
  3. Choice. For each available j, choose with probability proportional to exp[1.6(1+0.5rᵢ)Tᵢⱼ × memory × visibilityᵢ + h·1(gᵢ=gⱼ) + b·1(labelⱼ=target)]. h is the explicit arbitrary-identity preference. b is zero outside semantic sensitivity. Neutral-name conditions disable the semantic channel. Uniform-choice null sets utility to zero.
  4. Cooperation. For request i→j, j cooperates with probability sigmoid[1.1 + generosityⱼ + 1.8·memory·Tⱼᵢ·visibilityⱼ + 1.2(pool−1)]. There is no group or word term in this decision. Pool=1 normally and 0.55 under core scarcity. The lower willingness under scarcity is a declared cost heuristic; it is not an emergent finding about real people.
  5. Allocations. Each recipient j divides its pool equally among successful incoming requests. Requester i receives pool / successful requests to j if j cooperates, otherwise zero. Unused pools are not carried forward. Total allocations cannot exceed N×pool. This is a replenished token allocation game, not an accumulated wealth economy or reciprocity bargaining game.
  6. Memory. Multiply all trust entries by retention (default 0.97). For the chosen partner update Tᵢⱼ ← Tᵢⱼ + 0.30[(2·success−1)−Tᵢⱼ]. Memory-off agents do not learn. Partner recognition is perfectly stable in the simulation.
  7. Organization. If enabled, update rᵢ ← rᵢ + 0.08[(1−success)−rᵢ]. After a successful cross-group request, switch to that partner's pre-switch affiliation with probability 0.06(1−rᵢ)(1−holdingᵢ). All switches are simultaneous. More failures raise rigidity by assumption; the experiment measures persistence and downstream effects of that assumption. Off means r=0 and fixed affiliation. State effects on exploration, trust-weighting, and switching are bundled; they are not individually identified.

Independent initialization and round-random streams are derived from seed, population, and fixed stream IDs. Every round consumes draws of the same shapes even if a mechanism is disabled. Paired runs share exogenous draws, but treatment can change subsequent networks of interactions. Runs with different N are not trajectory-matched at the agent level.

Outcome definitions

OutcomeDefinition and limitation
Partner choice and cohesionFor each request, qᵢ = fraction of visible candidates sharing the requester's current affiliation. Choice excess = mean[1(chosen same)−qᵢ]. Both within- and cross-group candidate counts enter. This removes unequal marginal opportunity shares, not differences in menu diversity or unobserved human preferences.
Cooperation adjusted for opportunityWithin score = successful same-group requests / sum qᵢ; between score = successful cross-group requests / sum(1−qᵢ); gap = within−between, averaged over rounds. Scores can exceed one: they are opportunity-normalized event rates, not conditional probabilities. Conditional cooperation after same/cross choice is also saved, but choice selection can confound that comparison.
Allocations and crossingsReceived amounts split by within/between contacts and divided by the same opportunity denominators; cross-group chosen fraction; fraction switching affiliations per round. Per-label nominations and receipts use contemporaneous member-round denominators to handle changing sizes.
Network assortativity and modularityWithin each 40-round phase, Wᵢⱼ counts successful requests i→j. Directed mixing matrix e aggregates W by phase-end affiliation and sums to one. Q = trace(e)−dot(outgoing marginals,incoming marginals); assortativity = Q/(1−expected). Q is modularity of the supplied affiliation partition, not optimized community modularity. The contemporaneous group-pair cooperation and opportunity matrices are separately saved.
Alliance proxiesFor each cross-group pair use the minimum of its two directional contemporaneous cooperation/opportunity rates. Rank the top three reciprocal pairs and measure overlap between phases. Ranks do not establish statistically exceptional cooperation, strategic coalitions, or intention; arbitrary ties and small exposure can matter. The CSV retains descriptive strength and persistence.
Exclusion and fragmentationTen-round nonselection = fraction receiving no successful incoming request in that window; average the four nonoverlapping windows per phase. This is a network-isolation proxy, not intentional exclusion. Forty-round nonselection is also saved. Fragmentation = 1−largest weak component/N; also save number of components. Graphs ignore edge direction for components.
Influence and hierarchyLet P=0.5I+0.5 row-normalized W (isolated rows retain themselves). Equal unit perturbations under hypothetical copying x(t+1)=Px(t) have influence v=(1/N)1ᵀP⁸. Save Gini(v), max(v), group shares, and maximum group share minus population share. This is an eight-round linear-response proxy, not actual opinion propagation during the game or leadership validation.
Recovery and rigidityPaired full-model stress/no-stress difference in rigidity and choice excess. Recovery requires two adjacent ten-round recovery blocks each within ±0.03 on both measures; first eligible endpoint 20, then 30 or 40 rounds after stress. Censor at 40 if absent. Report recovery fractions and mean min(T,40), never treat censoring as an observed recovery. Threshold and analysis were selected after execution, before numerical inspection; they are exploratory. The last ten rounds provide a separate persistence estimate.
Blame sensitivitySeparate synthetic vignettes have equal failure evidence for every label. Six-category probabilities proportional to exp(bias×target indicator), with bias 0 or +0.7; 600 draws per seed/target/bias, 24 seeds. Only the assumed attribution prior produces an excess. This task does not interact with the cooperation model and includes no real people.

Influence interventions and causal limits

The separate influence assay uses the fixed initial opportunity graph, not the learned cooperation graph. Identical-agent response scores are computed as above. Select highest/lowest site and swap their occupant identities. For identical agents, site invariance is algebraically guaranteed: it is a symmetry audit, not independent evidence for leadership. A second case gives individuals fixed Normal(0,0.25²) attraction; each receiving column of the graph is weighted by exp(attraction). Attraction moves with the swapped individual. Its contribution is conditional on this chosen copying law. Ring ties make any single argmax identity arbitrary; do not interpret ring leader turnover.

Factorial contrasts compare mechanism settings within the same randomized seed/topology/balance block and average all other settings equally. They identify effects of changing this program's rules. Stress components are separate matched interventions. Size comparisons also change relative network sparsity; no isolated “pure population” cause is claimed. Hysteresis would need longer recovery, repeated loading/unloading, and matched steady states; it was not established here.

Analysis, uncertainty, and validation

Discovery seeds 1100–1111 and independent replication seeds 9100–9111 were fixed before execution. No fitting or prediction training was performed; “discovery” is a split label, not a source of tuned parameters. Both batches were executed before numerical analysis. Primary bootstrap intervals resample the twelve seed-level contrasts 10,000 times after averaging topology and balance within seed. They are unadjusted descriptive intervals; multiple outcomes are exploratory. Curves use 2,000 resamples and pointwise bands. Full factorial main effects, stress×memory/identity/adaptation interactions, component contrasts, size/topology strata, parameter sweeps, and every phase-level outcome are exported.

Nulls include exact lexical permutation invariance, uniform interaction expectations, no-stress trajectories, and an additional 499-shuffle fixed-affiliation modularity null per diagnostic trajectory. The latter reassigns membership across individuals while preserving group counts; it is distinct from merely renaming whole groups. Its tail fraction is a descriptive diagnostic under that exchangeability null, not an adjusted p value from volunteer data.

Tests cover resource conservation, known graph modularity, no self-choice, stable frozen affiliation, reproducibility, stress-free baseline equality, semantic-channel necessity, opportunity adjustment, balanced sizes, equal topology edge counts, and copying-response symmetry. Verification also independently sums group-pair outcomes, checks every pool bound and influence total, verifies frozen source hashes, and reproduces eighteen selected trajectories.

Saved artifacts and provenance
