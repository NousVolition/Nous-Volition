Optional human study · Draft for preregistration · Not executed

Arbitrary identities, words, and remembered interactions

Status: No recruitment, consent, participant data, ethics approval, or external registration exists for this protocol. The simulation results above are already known. This prospective human protocol must be dated and registered before collecting its confirmatory sample; the investigator, venue, ethics determination, compensation amount, and data-retention period must be filled in first.

Return to simulation report. This design separates arbitrary categorization from the meaning of words, and tests mild constraints without threats or real social exclusion.

Research questions and hypotheses

  1. Arbitrary identity: Do visible neutral group labels increase available-partner-adjusted same-group choice compared with undisplayed random group assignments?
  2. Label semantics: Do evocative labels change this outcome relative to neutral labels when task rules, colors, positions, and opportunities are held equivalent? Smoke-specific nominations are a prespecified secondary contrast, not a premise that Smoke is disliked.
  3. Constraints: Does a mild, reversible resource/time manipulation alter cooperation and group-boundary measures compared with a matched no-stress schedule? Direction is two-sided; the simulation's direction is not assumed to hold for humans.
  4. Displayed history: Does access to one's recent interaction record change cooperation and recovery? This manipulates an external memory aid; it cannot turn a person's memory off.

Participants, fixed sample, and stopping rule

Adults aged 18 or older who can give informed consent and understand the study language; recruit without selecting political, ethnic, religious, or other real-world identities. Exclude participation in another session of this study. Run a separate usability pilot of four sessions of 30 adults (N=120). Pilot data are never pooled with confirmatory outcomes. Use the pilot only to verify comprehension, timing, accessibility, and technical reliability; any design changes require an amended preregistration before the confirmatory cohort.

The proposed confirmatory budget is 72 sessions × 30 participants = 2,160 adults, six independent sessions per factorial cell. This is a planning choice, not a power guarantee. Stop at 72 completed, technically valid sessions, with at most 12 replacement sessions for the technical failures defined below. No stopping based on effect size, p values, label patterns, or interim hypothesis results. If recruitment or funding ends earlier, report the shortfall and analyze the achieved sample without calling it the planned study.

Power planning must use sessions as independent units. At an assumed session-outcome SD of 0.10, a neutral-versus-evocative marginal comparison with 24 sessions per level has SE≈0.0289. A conservative two-sided α=0.0125 threshold and 80% power gives an approximate detectable difference of 0.096. This normal approximation is illustrative, not an executed or validated power analysis. If that sensitivity is inadequate, increase the fixed session count in multiples of 12 before preregistration; do not extrapolate human power from the agent simulation.

Randomization and design

Session-level 3×2×2 factorial: displayed identity (hidden random groups / neutral Group 1–6 / Ice, Water, Fog, Mist, Gas (water vapor), Smoke), constraint schedule (no constraints / reversible constraints), and history display (off/on). Twelve cells, six sessions each. Randomize cells within recruitment batches using a concealed computer-generated schedule; record the seed and allocation file before recruitment. Prevent treatment spillover by running each session privately and prohibiting cross-session discussion during testing. Analyze assignment, regardless of participants' inferred identities.

Assign five members to each of six arbitrary groups uniformly at random, independently of seat, participant ID, and opportunities. In the hidden condition, keep the same latent memberships only for analysis; show no group badge. Rotate names across internal group IDs with a six-order Latin schedule within each factorial cell. Use identical typography and grayscale badges. Randomize menu positions every round. No adjectives describe the labels, and no facilitator calls Smoke dirty, dangerous, or an outsider. Groups have identical task rules and endowments in the core study.

Procedure and tasks

  1. Consent and practice, about 10 minutes. Explain arbitrary assignments, token tasks, mild time limits, recording, fixed payment, optional questions, and the right to withdraw. Give two private practice rounds and a comprehension check. Provide two explanation retries; anyone who still does not understand leaves with the promised base payment and is excluded before session assignment.
  2. Baseline, rounds 1–10. Each participant sees six eligible partners sampled uniformly without self-choice, chooses one, and decides whether to contribute one virtual token to that partner. Every participant makes at most one outgoing contribution per round. Keep identities stable across rounds. Record all menus; never fabricate choices or partner outcomes. Use a fixed 20-second decision window for both decisions together. If history is on, show the participant's own actions and received contributions from the last three rounds; if off, omit that panel. All participants see their actual current-round result. No public reputations or rankings.
  3. Manipulation, rounds 11–20. Constraint sessions use six task tokens instead of ten and an eight-second decision window. Controls retain ten tokens and twenty seconds. Tokens have no effect on guaranteed compensation and cannot create debt or loss of personal property. Candidate menus remain six items, so time pressure is not confounded with menu size. No threat, competition for payment, deception about emergencies, or public penalties. Timeouts are recorded as omissions, never recoded as rejection.
  4. Recovery, rounds 21–40. Restore ten tokens and twenty seconds in all sessions. Keep history assignments unchanged. Retain everyone in the game and permit a pause or withdrawal at any point. This is an experiment about recovery after constraints, not inducing distress.
  5. Private allocation task. At rounds 10, 20, 30 and 40, allocate ten purely experimental tokens among six presented partners (one from each latent group, uniformly sampled; no self). The hidden condition displays only IDs. Record the full choice set. Present instructions identically across conditions.
  6. Private ratings and debrief, about 5 minutes. Rate clarity, effort, perceived time pressure, and label familiarity/pleasantness after the behavioral task to avoid semantic priming. Skip options are allowed. Explain the arbitrary assignments and the actual hypotheses, and stress that no group has an intrinsic moral or personal meaning.

Frozen game rule: Each round starts with ten virtual tokens per participant (six during the constraint interval). Contributing one token costs the donor one token and credits the chosen recipient two virtual points. Retained tokens count as one point each. Thus round score = initial tokens − contribution + 2 × number of received contributions. Multiple incoming contributions add independently and require no response, so every person faces the same number of decisions. Points reset each round and have no monetary conversion; only the current score and incoming contributor IDs are shown privately. Choosing a partner and contributing zero is a valid decision. A timeout or skip has no transfer and is recorded as missing decision, not zero cooperation. This prospective human game differs from the simulation's recipient-response game; it tests the mechanisms rather than claiming an exact human replication. There are no fake messages or concealed penalties.

Separate semantics and fictional attribution module

Optional, separately preregistered semantic pilot: 120 different consenting adults rate all six words in randomized order for valence, familiarity, and expected behavior; include neutral group names as anchors. Do not preselect a disliked label after seeing results. Confirmatory Smoke contrasts stay two-sided. Semantic ratings are measured moderators, not randomized causes of behavior.

Optional safe attribution task: show a fictional six-team scheduling vignette with exactly equal error counts and exposure for all six teams. Randomly assign word-to-team mappings, balance order, and ask how responsibility for the fictional delay should be distributed. State explicitly that all teams are fictional; no participant receives blame or sees others' ratings. Primary attribution outcome is deviation from the evidence-based equal share, with the Smoke-minus-other-label contrast compared against its neutral counterpart. Analyze as a separate family with multiplicity control. Do not present blame vignettes before the core interaction task.

Primary outcomes and analysis

Primary outcome 1: each participant-round's same-group choice indicator minus the fraction of available candidates sharing that participant's current arbitrary membership. Compute participant means, then the session mean over rounds 11–20. Hidden-condition membership is a latent randomized analysis grouping. Report the same statistic in baseline and recovery. Primary outcome 2: proportion of eligible cooperative decisions with contribution, excluding technical missingness from denominators and reporting omissions separately.

Four prespecified confirmatory contrasts: neutral minus hidden on outcome 1; evocative minus neutral on outcome 1; constraint minus control on outcome 2; history display minus no history on outcome 2. Use session-level factorial regression with display indicators, constraint, history, all two-way interactions and their three-way terms, and recruitment-block indicators. Estimate marginal contrasts with equal weight over the other randomized factors. Use studentized randomization inference (10,000 allocations preserving the original blocked factorial schedule), with two-sided tests; report randomization-compatible or session-bootstrap confidence intervals. Apply Holm correction across the four primary tests at familywise α=0.05. Report adjusted and raw p values and effect sizes.

Secondary outcomes: conditional within/between cooperation, available-partner-adjusted allocations, switching if enabled only in a later module, group nomination and response shares, label-specific nominations (including Smoke), assortativity/Q, weak components, ten-round nonselection, reciprocal alliance proxies, and recovery. Test planned label contrasts in a separate Holm-adjusted family; all other secondary results are explicitly exploratory. Repeated rounds, six groups, or thirty people are not independent session replicates. Cluster by session in all auxiliary individual-level models. Report both effect estimates and uncertainty, including null or reversed effects.

Human recovery endpoint: subtract each session's baseline mean, then compare constrained and control sessions in each five-round recovery block. Recovery occurs at the second of two consecutive blocks whose estimated group difference lies within ±0.05 on both primary outcomes; require a confidence interval wholly inside the band for an equivalence claim. This population-level endpoint differs from the simulation's paired-trajectory rule. Report unresolved recovery at round 40 as censored, not permanent rigidity. The timeline and confidence intervals remain primary evidence if the threshold is not met.

Missingness, exclusions, and integrity

Analyze all randomized sessions with usable outcome logs. Exclude a session only for pre-documented platform failure that loses more than 20% of round records or reveals another condition's instructions; report its assignment and reason and retain a sensitivity analysis where possible. Replace it using the next allocation in the prespecified reserve schedule. Do not exclude a session because groups are uncooperative, labels are disliked, an effect reverses, or manipulation ratings are low.

Withdrawn participants contribute data only as permitted by consent and their deletion request. For retained data, analyze observed eligible opportunities and report denominators by condition. Record timeout, skip, withdrawal, and network failure as distinct events. Do not impute cooperation or hostility from missing responses. Report worst/best-case bounds for differential omissions, and flag substantial differential attrition as an identification limit. No inferential hypothesis inspection until the data freeze; technical monitoring may inspect logs without treatment labels.

Consent, safeguards, and debrief language

Suggested consent text: “You are invited to a study of decisions in small groups. Group assignments and names are random. You will choose partners and allocate experimental tokens. Some rounds have shorter decision times or fewer tokens. These tokens do not affect your guaranteed payment. You may skip optional questions, pause, or stop without penalty. We record game decisions and timing under a study ID. We will explain the study afterward. Please ask any questions before deciding whether to participate.” Add duration, contact, exact payment, privacy details, withdrawal/deletion process, and institutional information before use.

Suggested debrief: “The study tests whether arbitrary group names, access to past interactions, and mild task constraints affect decisions. No label describes your personality or any real-world community. All groups followed the same core rules. There were no real threats, and no group was assigned to be bad or deserving of blame. Any differences describe this particular task and do not rank people or groups.”

Obtain the appropriate independent ethics review or formal determination before recruitment; this document does not declare exemption. Use accessible timers, a pause option, private screens, and a neutral facilitator. Store only necessary pseudonymous events; keep consent/payment contact details separate. Do not collect protected demographic traits unless independently justified and consented. Define access controls and a retention/deletion date before registration. Share aggregated or carefully de-identified data, not a publicly identifiable social network. Follow the consent principles described by OHRP; requirements depend on the actual institution and jurisdiction.

Behavioral roles, switching, and leadership: future modules

C, roles: Randomize small differences in allowed partner reach, decision revision, or tie duration, counterbalanced across all six labels using Latin schedules. Cross role differences with label display; show the actual rules clearly. Separate each rule from any reputation description. Prespecify within-role label contrasts and between-role contrasts; do not infer a word effect from an assigned rule.

D, uncertainty: In a separate factorial module randomize disclosure of objective task information, keeping time and tokens fixed. Announce any missing information accurately; no false threats or fake majority opinions. Compare information-only, time-only, resource-only, and combined constraints against matched controls.

E, affiliation: Randomize optional, costless affiliation switching versus fixed groups; keep names and people distinct from organization. In the switching condition permit a private change after rounds 10, 20 and 30, including “no group.” No forced rejection or public expulsion. Record available destinations and exposure; assess whether earlier conditions predict voluntary retention after recovery.

Leadership: Randomize communication positions independently of label and participant, then rotate positions between blocks while keeping task information and opportunities otherwise matched. Compare nominations, actual uptake of suggestions, and success; add a separate ability measure obtained before assignment. This permits a test of position effects and conditional individual differences. It cannot equate a simulation centrality score with human authority.

These modules need their own fixed sample sizes, randomization schedules, outcomes, multiplicity families, and preregistrations; they are proposals, not completed experiments or implied parts of the 72-session core.
