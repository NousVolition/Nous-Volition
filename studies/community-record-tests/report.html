Which wording pairs keep the message?

We screened 1,011,773 DER STANDARD forum posts in the OFAI One Million Posts Corpus. Repeated posts were paired only within the same author, article and parent, at most 24 hours apart; newspaper staff were excluded. Of 520 disjoint changed-text candidates, 40 were chosen by a fixed hash order without consulting moderation status.

A single assistant reviewed the German headline, body, article title and available parent. 8 passed, 21 changed meaning, and 11 remained uncertain. Equivalence required preservation of the target, proposition, stance, negation, quantities, accusations and pragmatic force. Removing an insult or demand counted as a substantive change. Five passes changed words or syntax; three changed only typography.

The judgments were saved and hash-locked before reading statuses. This was outcome-blind review of the stored status fields; some texts themselves mention censorship or reposting. It is not independent human validation of meaning.

One lexical contrast added an indefinite article before an unpaid role: the earlier post was online and the later one deleted. Another exchanged two expressions of complete agreement while retaining the substantive argument: the earlier post was deleted and the later one online. These cases support a narrow observation of decision differences, conditional on the semantic review.

The identical-text control prevents a wording-only explanation. Its 193 differences include 179 online/deleted and 14 deleted/online pairs. Duplicate-post moderation is one possible explanation, but the source does not record deletion reasons. Neither unequal outcomes nor this directional pattern alone establishes bias, injustice or a particular policy.

Tightening the creation-time gap retains 3 differences among 7 meaning-preserving pairs within one hour, and 2 among 4 within ten minutes. The corresponding exact controls are 146/253 and 91/152. The eight accepted pairs come from eight authors and eight articles. Both non-top-level accepted pairs have their parent available; all eight article records are available. The whole evolving conversation and moderator exposure are not fixed.

Are later penalties more severe?

The public Cornell Wikipedia block log yields 57,600 eligible accounts with a finite first observed block and complete 180-day follow-up. Of 18,784 distinct second blocks in that window, 10,097 initially assigned an indefinite duration; 8,687 finite pairs enter the duration comparison. These are initial assigned lengths, not time actually served. Modifications to an active block are not counted as separate encounters.

This extends the previously reported 8,687-pair analysis with exact nonempty reason-string matching. Normalization only decodes HTML, collapses whitespace and ignores case; dates, evidence, links and other content remain. The same-moderator comparison excludes missing moderator identities.

In the strictest group, 87.5% had a longer second penalty, and the median account-level ratio was 3.5× [3.0, 4.29]. Its separate first/second medians are 31 and 168 hours; their ratio is not the median paired ratio. An exact sign test excluding 37 ties gives p = 2.51 × 10−109. That tests directional symmetry in this selected cohort, not whether prior punishment caused escalation. First and second log-durations correlate (Spearman ρ = 0.573). Accounts contribute one pair; the bootstrap resamples whole accounts 2,000 times with a fixed seed. It does not model dependence through shared moderators.

Escalation is supported; a causal memory mechanism remains open. Equal reason text does not guarantee equal conduct or severity. These records condition on being blocked again, omit unpenalized encounters and do not manipulate whether history is visible. Current behavior, policy, reporting and selection can contribute. No social-memory parameter is fitted from this association.

People can be Water, Fog, Smoke or Clay in different situations, combine these descriptions or reject them. Preserve strong descriptions of force, resistance, exclusion and adaptation when they describe the observation or an attributed account. The public-record results add evidence about treatment; they do not turn these metaphors into fixed types of people.
