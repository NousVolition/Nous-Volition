# Adoption, ancestry and transmitted history: reference only

Requested worldwide scope; reviewed 22 October 2026. This reference informs distinctions in the model, not parameter fitting. No adoptee is labeled an anomaly. An adopted person's biological ancestry can differ from the family transmitting caregiving, stories, culture and lived experience. Their descendants may receive influences through both routes. DNA inheritance is not evidence that specific autobiographical memories are inherited. Unknown biological history is unknown, not zero.

## Worldwide numbers and their coverage

The [HCCH statistics page](https://www.hcch.net/en/publications-and-studies/details4/?dtid=32&pid=5891) links Peter Selman's February 2024 compilation, [Global Statistics for Intercountry Adoption, 2004–2022](https://assets.hcch.net/docs/a8fe9f19-23e6-40c2-855e-388e112bf1f5.pdf). Table 1 reports 45,482 intercountry adoptions in 2004 and 3,700 in 2022. The table spans 25 receiving states, with annual reporting coverage varying; both endpoint years show 23 reporting states. `adoption_reference.csv` transcribes the annual TOTAL and number-of-states rows. Periods in the source table are thousands separators.

These are **intercountry flows within the table's reporting coverage**, not all worldwide adoptions, the number of people living as adoptees, or a prevalence of trauma. Domestic adoptions are absent. Fiscal-year and calendar-year reporting and revisions differ; the source flags incomplete/suppressed small counts and some provisional figures. Source and receiving-country counts must not be added together as independent adoptions. This is a clearly dated global-scope compilation, not a claim that 2022 is the newest national information available or that a complete current world total was found.

## What adoption research can help separate

[Leve et al. 2019, the Early Growth and Development Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31526412/), reports a US prospective study of 561 adoptees with birth and adoptive families. Its design examines genetic, prenatal, caregiving and broader contextual influences. It supports keeping those routes separate in an investigation; the cohort size is not a worldwide count and the study does not establish inherited event memories. Selective placement, prenatal exposure, contact between families, timing, culture, and socioeconomic conditions still need attention in causal interpretations.

For this Hug test, this distinction means:

- The origin ledger says who directly experienced a fictional event and who received an account.
- Caregiving and neighbor routes can change independently of biological ancestry.
- A later generation can retain an attachment to a story without being assigned the earlier person's lived experience.
- Unknown ancestry is recorded as unavailable. The uncalibrated biological-transfer mechanism is disabled, not concluded biologically absent.
- The stronger-transmission and opposing-feedback scenarios test parameter sensitivity. Their frequency is not estimated, and they do not classify adopted people, descendants of enslaved people, or people who depart from social norms.

## Trauma and biological inheritance

Genetic inheritance concerns DNA sequence; epigenetic mechanisms concern regulation and are not interchangeable with inherited narratives. [Horsthemke 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-05445-5) explains how genetic, ecological and cultural transmission confound claims of human germline epigenetic inheritance. [Yehuda and Lehrner 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6127768/) reviews trauma-related intergenerational findings and their limitations. These do not supply a universal transmission fraction, a generation-by-generation decay rate, or evidence that an unknown ancestor's personal memories are accessible to a descendant.

The computational .55 and .9 factors therefore refer only to hypothetical communicated influence. Neither comes from these studies. A biological extension would require a specified measured phenotype, prospective family/exposure data, distinction between prenatal and germline exposure, and controls for ongoing social transmission. Adoption totals cannot identify that mechanism.

## Useful future comparisons

Compare recorded exposure histories and later outcomes while distinguishing direct experience, communicated accounts, caregiving continuity/changes, prenatal exposure and biological relatedness. Record supportive contexts and variation as well as adversity. Do not infer harmful intent, trauma severity or nonconformity from ancestry or adoption status. The present study contains no human participant records and tests no clinical predictions.
